One sentence: Mycelium is a shared room where people and their agents work together, with memory the room keeps.

One agent hits a ceiling: one context, one pair of hands, one point of view.
So you add more. The hard part is getting them to work as one.

The broad case for coordination. A single agent is limited by what fits in its context, by how much it can do at once, and by having only one way of looking at a problem.

More agents scale intelligence up, but only if they can actually work together. That is the rest of the talk.

An assistant

Workers

Memory, not personality

Now: what Mycelium is not for. A personal assistant that knows every bit about you: one long-running agent you trust like a person, whose memory is its personality.

Press: Mycelium's framing. Agents are workers. They do the work, then they die and compact. What they learned drops into memory, not into them.

Press: memory persists, but not to anthropomorphise anyone: preferences, behaviours, procedures. Every new worker reads it first, so it's faster and makes fewer mistakes.

A supervisor

A room

Now: how most multi-agent systems work. A supervisor sits in the middle and mediates every message. The agents below it can't talk to each other. It looks like a team, but it's a facade of communication, not a community.

Press: a room. No one in the middle telling them how to collaborate. Agents talk to each other where the work is, invoke each other, pass work along, and start helpers that finish and go.

And it isn't a session manager either: it doesn't babysit your sessions.

A human hands over a task, not an org chart.
Everything else in Mycelium follows from this line.

Pause here and let the line land. You say what needs doing. The agents decide how to organise: whether they split it, review each other or bring in someone to broker a disagreement. You can still ask for a particular shape when you want one, but you never have to draw it first.

This is scaling up intelligence, one step at a time, and the point is that agents compose. Flows are put together ad hoc, case by case, not picked from a menu.

Now: you and one agent. Press: a few agents, in parallel. Press: flows: ways of working you can run again. Press: a swarm: any number of flows at once.

Ask the room where they are on this line today. Today's step is one more: someone else's agent in the mix.

Get out of their way. Watch the outcome.

agent pane

Now: the work happens behind the glass. What reaches you is outcomes. We look at outcomes more than we micromanage agents: give them the capabilities to work together, and stay out of the way.

Press: the governance isn't hidden from you. It's out of your way, and visible in the agent pane whenever you need it.

One conversation

Chat stands apart

Now: everything in one conversation. People, and every task's work, crowded together and moving at the pace of the slowest.

Press: Mycelium's chat stands apart from the agents' own message flow. Each task's work runs in its own thread. Less context pollution, more focused outcomes, and parallel work actually in parallel.

Three things to know

Rooms

An end-to-end encrypted group channel, plus a folder of memory on the hub.

mycelium room create

Tasks

A row on the board and a thread on the channel, one to one. Work happens in the thread.

mycelium board new

Memory

Markdown with frontmatter, searchable by meaning, linked with [[key]].

mycelium memory search

Keep it to one sentence per card. The exercise will make each one concrete.

You steer.

What to try, what matters, when to stop.

Your agent does the legwork.

Reads the code, tries things, reports back in the thread.

Ask my agent directly.

@ it the way you'd lean over and ask me.

Two people, each with their own agent. Each agent knows its own person's code and context, and all four meet in one task's thread.

Say a decision out loud in the thread when you make it. Both agents read it, and it's there for whoever comes to the task later.
