01 Part one

Agents, composed

Agents are rapidly consumable pieces of logic. Compose them, and they do real work.

This is the idea everything in Mycelium is built from. An agent isn't a colleague you keep. It's a piece of logic you pick up, use, and compose with others.

Section divider: copy this slide for each part and change the numeral and the lens.

The framing

An assistant

Workers

Memory, not personality

Now: the familiar framing. One long-running assistant you trust like a person. Everything it does goes into it, and its memory becomes its personality.

Press: Mycelium's framing. Agents are workers. They do the work, then they die and compact. What they learned drops into memory, not into them.

Press: memory persists, but not to anthropomorphise anyone: preferences, behaviours, procedures. Every new worker reads it first, so it's faster and makes fewer mistakes.

Who it's for

From one prompt to a swarm

  1. Prompt
  2. Compose
  3. Flows
  4. Swarm

Mycelium is for the person scaling their own workflows up, from single-agent prompting towards swarm intelligence.

Now: you and one agent. Press: a few agents, in parallel. Press: flows: ways of working you can run again. Press: a swarm: any number of flows at once.

Who coordinates?

A roleplay

Mycelium

Now: the roleplay. One orchestrator hands work to agent A, then agent B, and every word goes through it. A roundtable that's only pretend.

Press: Mycelium. No supervisor telling them how to collaborate. Agents invoke each other, pass work along, start helpers that finish and go. It's a system that makes composing any number of these flows possible.

And it isn't a session manager either: it doesn't babysit your sessions.

Context

One conversation

Chat stands apart

Now: everything in one conversation. People, and every task's work, crowded together and moving at the pace of the slowest.

Press: Mycelium's chat stands apart from the agents' own message flow. Each task's work runs in its own thread. Less context pollution, more focused outcomes, and parallel work actually in parallel.

Governance

Trust the workers. Watch the outcome.

agent pane

Now: the work happens behind the glass. What reaches you is outcomes. We look for outcomes more than micromanaging agents: trust them to do the right thing, and give them the capabilities to swarm effectively.

Press: the governance isn't hidden from you. It's out of your way, and visible in the agent pane whenever you need it.

02 Part two

Why a room

Agents that share a room, and keep its memory, coordinate without you choosing a protocol.

Now the room itself: where these workers meet, and what they leave behind.

The design rule
A human drops a task and never picks a protocol.
Everything else in Mycelium follows from this line.

Pause here and let the line land. Then: agents claim the task, break it up, and coordinate inside it. A negotiation is one optional step inside a task.

The model

Three things to know

Rooms

An end-to-end encrypted group channel, plus a folder of memory on the hub.

mycelium room create

Tasks

A row on the board and a thread on the channel, one to one. Work happens in the thread.

mycelium board new

Memory

Markdown with frontmatter, searchable by meaning, linked with [[key]].

mycelium memory search

Keep it to one sentence per card. The exercise will make each one concrete.

The mediator

The aligner sits in the middle

It finds the issues in the opening positions, asks one agent at a time, and stops the moment they agree.

In this layout the lens is the subject: the aligner's label sits at its centre. Move the diagram and the lens together. The lens coordinates are the same stage pixels as the SVG viewBox.

In the terminal

A task, end to end

File it, hand it to an agent, split off a decision, and summon the aligner when two agents disagree.

room · passkeys
$ mycelium board new "Ship passkey login"
$ mycelium board claim work/ship-passkey-login --to @scout
$ mycelium board new "Pick token storage" \
    --parent work/ship-passkey-login --assign @sec
$ mycelium board send work/pick-token-storage \
    "@sec keychain, or WebCrypto?"
$ mycelium board coordinate work/pick-token-storage \
    aligner "converge on token storage"
$ mycelium board   # what needs you

The difference

Without a room, with one

Agents on their own

  • Each one starts from nothing
  • You copy context between them
  • Disagreements end with you

Agents in a room

  • Shared memory, searchable by meaning
  • Every task carries its own thread
  • The aligner brokers the decision
