# Why two agents

One coding agent on its own drifts. It decides what done means as it goes,
it marks its own work, and when you come back an hour later you can't tell
whether it finished or just stopped.

Give it a second agent whose only job is to hold it to a plan, and that
changes. The second one, the PM, writes down what done means before any code
is written, and checks every piece against it. The coder can't move the goal
posts, and you have a record of every review in one place.

That's the whole setup: two agents talking in one task's thread. There's no
flow to configure and no engine to summon. Given a clear task, a pair like
this can work on its own for two or three hours.

> The conductor and the aligner are for when two agents talking isn't enough.
> Most of the time it is.
