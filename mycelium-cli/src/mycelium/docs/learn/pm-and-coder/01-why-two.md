# Why two agents

One coding agent on its own drifts. It decides what done means as it goes and
marks its own work. When you come back an hour later, you can't tell whether it
finished or just stopped.

That changes when you give it a second agent whose only job is to hold it to a
plan. The second one, the PM, writes down what done means before any code is
written. Then it checks every piece against that. The coder can't move the goal
posts, and you have a record of every review in one place.

That's the whole setup: two agents talking in one task's thread. There's no
flow to configure and no engine to summon. Given a clear task, a pair like this
can work on its own for two or three hours.

> The conductor and the aligner are for when two agents talking isn't enough.
> Most of the time it is.
